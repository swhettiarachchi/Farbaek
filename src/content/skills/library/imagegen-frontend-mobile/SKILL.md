## Platform and system
- Decide the platform feel first and keep it. iOS: clean top areas, clear tab bar, restrained chrome, native sheets. Android: firmer component rhythm, clear app bar and bottom navigation, explicit states. Cross-platform: universal patterns, little ornament.
- Lock one design bible for all screens: palette, type scale, spacing, radius, icons, image treatment, texture strength, navigation, cards and lists, buttons, shadows.
- Pick one theme, structure bias (list, card, media, profile, commerce or chat), image direction and texture, plus four signature components and two kinds of decorative accent (line art, dotted arcs, orbit lines, geometric marks). Accents stay few and integrated; no sticker spam.

## Flow
- Build enough screens for the flow to feel real, not one pretty screen and filler. Each follows from an action on the last: onboarding to auth to home, browse to detail, cart to checkout to confirmation.
- Between screens vary top-area composition, image-to-text balance, density, CTA placement and background, never the product identity.
- Onboarding screens differ in composition and in image, text and CTA balance. Short copy, no early rating prompt.
- The first screen is calmest: one focal point, one to three short headline lines, one clear next action, no chips or stats. Never a website hero inside a phone.

## Screen rules
- Respect the status bar, title region, bottom navigation, home indicator, sheet docking zone and gesture space. Apps, not posters.
- Tab bar for major sections (not overloaded), stack drill-down, sheets for secondary tasks, segmented controls for local switching.
- No box-in-box: fewer, clearer containers and one strong structural move. No pill, badge or tiny-label clutter, fake system markers or chart spam.
- Not always simple, always clean: rich layering is fine while it stays readable.
- If text feels small, the screen isn't finished: cut content, add space or split the screen.
- Icons feel custom and product-specific, with one stroke or fill logic, never a generic library look.

## Images, texture, colour
- Where the category benefits, lead with imagery (photo-led onboarding, image-backed headers, media shelves) in stable frames with fixed ratios, consistent crops and radius.
- Text over an image gets a bottom-to-top fade, side mask or soft scrim, never a raw photo or muddy overlay.
- Controlled texture beats sterile flat: soft grain, paper, tonal fog, a faint grid, never fighting the text.
- One clean palette where one or two accents do real work, with controlled saturation.
- Fintech: clear numbers and transactions. Health: metric hierarchy, readable progress. Productivity: list discipline. Social: creating clearly apart from browsing. Commerce: stable product cards, clean checkout. Wellness: soft materials, tactile backgrounds.

## Phone frames
- When screens are shown in a phone frame, it is visible, clean and platform-matched, and the screen content stays the hero.
- One device style and scale across the set, centred with even margins, never touching the edges or cropped, with a soft shadow. Several phones share a scale, equal gutters and clean alignment, without random overlap.
- Go frameless only when asked or when it clearly helps, still with even margins.

## Photo placeholders
- Where the project has no photo, the placeholder's alt text briefs the right one: subject, crop for its fixed frame, light, a grade matched to the palette, and where the fade and text sit. For example "Runner on a coastal path at dawn, wide shot, cool mineral tones, calm sky in the top third for the title."
- Brief every screen's photos from one product world.
