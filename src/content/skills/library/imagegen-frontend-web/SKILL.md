## Direction
- The brief overrides these defaults: bold but controlled, airy, strongly image-led, every section with a job (hook, proof, educate, convert).
- Minimalist: mini hero, solid or softly textured surfaces. Editorial: side images, duotone, off-grid offsets. Cinematic or luxury: giant hero over a full-bleed photo. SaaS or fintech: mid hero, product asset on a solid surface. Agency: giant or mini, never in between. Shop: product-led, unmistakable buy actions.
- Commit to one theme (pristine light, deep dark, bold studio solid or quiet premium neutral), one type character, one section system, four signature components and two motion moods (scrubbed text reveal, pinned narrative, staggered rise, parallax drift, fade-through).
- Thread one concept spine through visuals and copy: artifact, journey, precision tool, living system, stage or archive.
- Place exactly one second-read moment: an asymmetric bleed, one structural oversized numeral, one material switch, a side-rail note, or a macro crop in the brand colour.

## Hero
- Text left, image right is the most overused AI hero. Prefer centered low over a full-bleed image, text bottom-left or bottom-right over an image, stacked center, off-grid, or the inverted split.
- One scale: giant statement, mid editorial, or mini minimalist (confident restraint).
- Headline of 5 to 10 strong words, short support, one obvious next action. No pills, fake stats or badges.
- Medium or light weights, tight tracking, few lines, strong scale contrast. No gradient text.

## Section rhythm
- Use at least three composition anchors, none more than twice in a row.
- Vary backgrounds: solid with an inline asset, paper or grid texture, full-bleed photo with overlay, 60/40 side image, colour block with a detail crop, tonal gradient, duotone, colour-blocked diptych. No mode more than three times in a row. Unless the brief is minimal, include one full-bleed section and one mini minimalist section.
- Mix large, medium and mini sections, but keep the gaps even and slightly more generous than default. Calm sections separate dense ones.
- Vary CTA style at least once (pill, ghost, underlined link with arrow, full-width banner). The primary stays unmistakable; the last section closes with one strong action and a trust cue.
- Bento grids have no accidental gaps; product UI stacks tell a product story, never a fake dashboard.

## Images, palette, surfaces
- Images are structure, not decoration. Several sections carry imagery, with at least two distinct crops (macro and context, portrait and wide). No tiny thumbnails, no one image then walls of text.
- One palette: primary, secondary, a sparing accent and neutrals. Sections reuse it; never swap themes per section.
- Subtle palette-matched gradients and vignettes are welcome; rainbow mesh, purple-to-blue and purposeless glow are not.
- Don't retreat to plain white where a section needs atmosphere. Full-bleed photos match the palette, with an overlay that keeps text readable.
- Charts only when the brand needs them; otherwise human proof (quotes, timelines, real workflow screenshots). No three identical stat columns.

## Photo placeholders
- Where the project has no photo, the placeholder's alt text is the brief for the right one: subject, crop, light, a grade matched to the palette, and where the text-safe area sits. For example "Hands shaping clay on a wheel, close crop, warm side light, sand tones, clear space top left for the headline."
- Brief one image treatment across the site; no stock cliches or irrelevant scenery.

## Consistency
Every page shares the palette, type scale, CTA family, radius language, image treatment and tone, so it reads as one site.
