# Tokens, not servers, size every plan

Running a Claude Code or Antigravity-style platform in October 2026 costs about **$80 a month in fixed infrastructure** (Vercel Pro, Supabase Pro, Resend and Paddle's payout wire). Almost every other dollar grows with use. One measured agent session costs about **2 cents on Claude Haiku 5.5, 36 cents on Sonnet 5.5, 71 cents on Opus 5.5 and 26 cents on Gemini 3.8 Flash** at its January 2027 price, and a Vercel sandbox costs about **15 cents an hour**. With Paddle's 5% + 50¢ fee and a 40% margin on price, each subscriber can cost at most **0.55 × price − $0.50**. That is $4.99 at $9.99, $10.49 at $19.99, $27.00 at $50, $43.49 at $79.99, $54.50 at $100, $109.50 at $200 and $164.50 at $300. Spread the fixed stack over an assumed 200 paying subscribers and give about 14% of each budget to sandbox time, and the remaining model budget buys roughly **10, 23, 63, 103, 130, 263 and 397 Sonnet 5.5 sessions** (about half that on Opus 5.5) plus 5 to 150 sandbox hours. Put another way, every dollar of model usage at list price has to sell for $2.12 to $2.71. That is far less usage per dollar than GitHub Copilot ($15 to $200 of credit for $10 to $100) or Replit and v0 (credit at full face value) include, because model owners and volume buyers don't pay list prices. A list-price reseller cannot win on raw volume, so it has to win on predictability. That means sizing on Gemini's post-increase prices (Argon doubles on December 1, 2026 and 3.8 Flash on January 1, 2027), metering in a published unit, offering a two-month rollover (which costs no margin when allowances are costed at full use), selling top-ups at the plan rate, and including the sandboxes, hosting and security checks that rivals keep for business tiers. The session costs rest on a single Anthropic sample session with an assumed 30% tokenizer uplift, and several competitor figures are secondary or unverified. Both caveats are flagged where they apply.

## Fixed infrastructure costs about $80 a month, or 40 cents a subscriber

The platform cannot run on free tiers. Vercel restricts Hobby to non-commercial personal use and counts processing payments as commercial use, so a paid product needs Pro or Enterprise ([Vercel Fair Use Guidelines](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). **Vercel Pro is a $20 monthly platform fee.** It includes one deploying seat, $20 of usage credit that expires at month end, and the base Flat Rate CDN tier of 1 million requests and 1 TB of transfer. Extra deploying seats cost $20 each ([Vercel Pro Plan docs](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan)). If preview traffic outgrows that tier, the next one is $20 a month for 10 million requests and 50 TB ([Flat Rate CDN docs](https://vercel.com/docs/pricing/flat-rate-cdn)). **Supabase Pro is $25 a month per organization.** That price includes $10 of compute credit, which covers one Micro instance ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Quotas include 100,000 auth MAU, 250 GB egress and 8 GB of disk per project, with overage at $0.00325 per MAU and $0.09 per GB ([Billing on Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-on-supabase)). **Resend Pro is $20 a month for 50,000 emails** ([Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)). At an assumed 30 transactional emails per subscriber, that covers about 1,600 subscribers. Paddle sends payouts monthly, and a cross-currency SWIFT wire costs **$15 per payout** ([Paddle payout fees](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/is-there-a-fee-taken-for-payouts)). The fixed stack is therefore $80 a month. That is **$1.60 per subscriber at 50 subscribers and $0.40 at 200**. At 1,000 subscribers it is about $0.15, assuming Supabase moves to Medium compute (a net $50 more) and the CDN moves to the $20 tier, for about $150 in total ([Supabase: Manage Compute usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/compute)). The tables below assume 200 subscribers and an equal share per subscriber, and they treat Vercel's $20 credit as a buffer rather than a saving. They leave out costs the research did not price: domains, monitoring, accounting and company costs.

One architectural choice can break this arithmetic. If every user's generated app gets its own Supabase project, each project adds Micro compute at $0.01344 an hour, about **$9.81 a month**, and only the first project is offset by the credit ([Supabase: Compute and Disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)). Supabase's Spend Cap does not cover compute, so the platform would have to enforce the limit itself ([Supabase: Cost control](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/cost-control)). One always-on project per user would cost more than the entire $4.99 budget of the $9.99 plan. The options are a single shared multi-tenant project, or projects that pause when idle, since paused projects are not billed for compute ([Supabase: Manage Compute usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/compute)).

Sandboxes are a per-use cost, not a fixed one. On Pro in the default `iad1` region, **Vercel Sandbox charges $0.128 per active vCPU-hour, $0.0212 per provisioned GB-hour of memory and $0.60 per million creations**. Packages and repositories the sandbox downloads are free, egress is covered by the Flat Rate CDN tier, snapshots cost $0.08 per GB-month, sessions run up to 24 hours, and Pro allows 10,000 concurrent sandboxes ([Vercel Sandbox pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/sandbox/pricing)). From those rates, the default 2 vCPU, 4 GB sandbox costs **$0.110 an hour at 10% CPU use, $0.149 at 25% and $0.341 at 100%**. Memory is billed whether or not the agent is working, so it is about 77% of the cost at low utilization. Idle sandboxes therefore need to stop quickly, and Vercel's 5-minute default timeout helps. This report plans at $0.149 an hour (25% CPU) and allows 2 GB of snapshot storage ($0.16 a month) per subscriber. E2B is the obvious alternative. It charges a $150 monthly Pro base fee and about $0.166 per running hour for the same size ([E2B pricing](https://e2b.dev/pricing)). By this arithmetic Vercel is cheaper below roughly 32% CPU utilization, but E2B's billing documentation was not checked to confirm that it bills allocated rather than active CPU.

Payments are where location matters. Stripe does not list Sri Lanka among its supported countries ([Stripe global](https://stripe.com/global)), and Stripe Managed Payments accepts businesses from 37 listed countries that do not include Sri Lanka ([Stripe Managed Payments eligibility](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility)). Lemon Squeezy's owner says it intends to move users to Managed Payments ([Lemon Squeezy 2026 update](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/2026-update)). Paddle supports Sri Lanka ([Paddle supported countries](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)). **It charges 5% + 50¢ per transaction, covering payments, billing, global tax remittance, fraud and buyer support** ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). Paddle calculates that fee on the total including tax ([Paddle earnings example](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/reporting/how-are-my-earnings-calculated)). Chargebacks cost $20 each and the fee is kept even when Paddle wins ([Paddle chargebacks](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/risk-prevention/understanding-chargebacks-with-paddle)). Payouts go out monthly above a $100 threshold ([Paddle payouts](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/when-and-how-do-i-get-paid)), and Paddle can take an FX margin of up to 1.5% when the payout currency differs from the balance currency ([Paddle payout fees](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/is-there-a-fee-taken-for-payouts)). A US company through Stripe Atlas ($500 one-time) would unlock Stripe direct at about 4.1% to 6.6% + 30¢, but that beats Paddle only on US cards, and the founder would then carry tax registration, filing and liability ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). The research did not cover Sri Lankan exchange-control rules or whether Atlas accepts Sri Lankan residents.

## One agent session costs 2 cents to $1.48, and caching decides where you land

The only published, itemised agent session in the notes comes from Anthropic's Claude Code cost guide. It used 1.2K input tokens, 50K cache-write tokens, 940K cache-read tokens and 5.3K output tokens, which cost $0.55 on Sonnet 4.6 over 6 minutes 20 seconds of API time ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). Re-pricing those exact tokens at current list prices gives the table below. Claude prices come from [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) and Gemini prices from [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), which was last updated October 16, 2026. The planning column adds 30% to Claude models, because Anthropic says Claude 4.7 and later tokenizers produce about 30% more tokens for the same text, and the sample was measured on the older tokenizer ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). For Gemini, the planning column uses the post-increase price.

| Model | Input / output per MTok | Cache write (5 min) / cache read | Sample session at list | Planning cost per session | Scheduled change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Fable 5.1 | $10 / $50 | $12.50 / $0.25 | $1.14 | **$1.48** | None announced |
| Claude Opus 5.5 | $4 / $20 | $5 / $0.20 | $0.55 | **$0.71** | None announced |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 | $2 / $10 | $2.50 / $0.10 | $0.27 | **$0.36** | None announced |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 (prompt up to 100K) | $0.10 / $0.50 | $0.125 / $0.01 | $0.018 | **$0.024** | 5x rates on prompts over 100K |
| Gemini 4 Argon | $2 / $10, then $4 / $20 | Implicit cache read $0.10, then $0.20 | $0.25, then $0.50 | **$0.50** | Doubles December 1, 2026 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash | $0.75 / $3.75, then $1.50 / $7.50 | Read $0.075, then $0.15 | $0.13, then $0.26 | **$0.26** | Doubles January 1, 2027 |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | $0.30 / $2.50 | Read $0.03 | $0.057 | **$0.057** | None listed |

The session is about a million tokens. **Cache reads make up 94% of the tokens but only about a third of the cost.** On Sonnet 5.5, cache writes are 46% of the session cost, cache reads 34% and output 19%. The same tokens sent without caching would cost $2.04 on Sonnet 5.5, 7.4 times the cached price. Caching is therefore the largest single cost lever, ahead of model choice. Anthropic notes that Claude Code uses one-hour caches on subscriptions and five-minute caches on API keys, and that a message after a longer break reprocesses the full context ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). In a builder, users often pause to review a preview for longer than five minutes. One-hour writes cost 2x input instead of 1.25x and raise the Opus 5.5 sample session from $0.55 to $0.70, but they avoid repeated writes ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Several other rules matter for costing. Thinking tokens bill as output and cannot be turned off on the 5.x models ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). Haiku 5.5 bills the whole request at five times the rate once a prompt passes 100K tokens, so it is cheap only for short subagent calls or compacted contexts. Batch's 50% discount only helps asynchronous background work. Priority Tier can no longer be bought and does not cover any 5.x model ([Anthropic service tiers](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/service-tiers)). One scheduled increase has already been cancelled: Anthropic dropped Sonnet 5's planned rise to $3 / $15 on September 1, 2026 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)).

Averages hide most of the risk. Anthropic reports that enterprise Claude Code users average **about $13 per developer per active day and $150 to $250 per month**, with 90% under $30 per active day. It does not say which models those figures assume ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). Agent teams use about 7x the tokens of a normal session. A 2026 study of eight frontier models on SWE-bench Verified found that agentic tasks use about 1,000 times more tokens than code chat, and that runs of the same task differ by **up to 30x** ([arXiv 2604.22750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750)). In practice, a flat allowance counted in "sessions" will be wrong for individual users. The allowance should be metered in cost terms and shown as an estimated session count. It also means a full-time professional at Anthropic's enterprise average needs a price of **$274 to $455 a month** just to cover $150 to $250 of model cost under this margin rule. Twenty-one active days at $13 is about $273 of cost, which needs a price near $500.

## Every plan can spend 55% of its price minus 50 cents

With a 40% margin on price and Paddle's fee, the cost budget per subscriber is C = P × (0.60 − 0.05) − $0.50. Equivalently, the price for a given cost is P = (C + 0.50) / 0.55, which matches the formula in the payment research ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). The first table shows how each price breaks down. The column headed "if 40% means markup" shows the alternative reading once, for comparison: price after fees equals 1.4 times cost. That reading allows about 23% to 29% more cost per subscriber, but it leaves only 28.6% of net revenue as profit.

Haiku counts assume every prompt stays under 100K tokens; above that they fall to about a fifth. At the top end, the $200 plan covers about 63 Fable 5.1 sessions or 188 Gemini 4 Argon sessions (at the December price), and the $300 plan about 95 Fable sessions. Only **37% of the $9.99 price and about 47% of the $300 price** can go to model tokens. At full use, a Sonnet session costs the subscriber $1.00 on the $9.99 plan and $0.76 on the $300 plan. Sandbox time is cheap by comparison. At Vercel's own 10% utilization assumption, the same dollars buy 35% more hours (67 instead of 50 on the $100 plan). The budgets above also assume no free tier, since every free user's tokens come out of the 40%.

Four sensitivities change these numbers materially. The first is subscriber count. At 50 subscribers the fixed share rises to $1.60, and the $9.99 plan falls to about 6 Sonnet sessions. At 1,000 it falls to $0.15, and the plan reaches 11. The $100 plan barely moves (126 to 131). The $9.99 plan is therefore only workable as a Haiku 5.5 and Flash-Lite plan. Paddle takes 10% of that price but offers bespoke pricing for orders under $10, so it is worth asking before launch ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). The second is tax. If prices are shown without tax and 20% VAT is added at checkout (an illustrative rate, not a sourced one), Paddle's fee on the larger total costs another 1% of price, for example $10.29 instead of $10.49 at $19.99. If prices include tax, the VAT comes out of the budget and the $19.99 plan drops to $8.50. Tax-exclusive pricing protects the margin. The third is Gemini's schedule. A $19.99 plan sized on today's Flash price would promise 65 Flash sessions. From January 1 those cost $18.79 with sandbox and fixed costs, against $18.49 left after Paddle, which turns a 40% margin into **a 1.5% loss** for Flash-heavy users. A $100 plan sized on today's Argon price would lose 6.3% from December 1. The fourth is annual billing. The market discounts annual plans by roughly 10% to 28%. Replit takes up to 10% off ([Replit pricing](https://replit.com/pricing)), Bolt up to 28% ([Bolt pricing](https://bolt.new/pricing)), Lovable gives two months free ([Lovable subscription plans](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/subscription-plans)) and Claude Pro is $17 instead of $20 ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). Offering two months free with the same monthly allowance cuts the margin to **about 30%**. Keeping 40% means cutting the allowance by 13% to 16%, for example a $9.12 monthly budget instead of $10.49 on the $19.99 plan. Annual plans also lock in the cost exposure through the Gemini increases, so their rate card must use 2027 prices.

The pattern is that **vendors who own models, or buy at volume, include more usage than their sticker price**. Copilot's credits are priced at each model's listed token rates, and its plans include 1.5x (Pro), 1.8x (Pro+) and 2x (Max) their price in credits ([Copilot billing docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing/usage-based-billing-for-individuals)). Replit frames its fee as credit at full face value ([Replit pricing](https://replit.com/pricing)). v0 publishes per-model rates and adds daily login credits on top of plan credit ([v0 pricing](https://v0.app/pricing)). Cursor subsidises its own Grok and Composer models inside the plan and passes Claude and GPT through at API list price on prepaid top-ups ([Cursor pricing docs](https://cursor.com/docs/account/pricing)). Google draws Antigravity's Gemini pool down at API prices, so cheaper models stretch further ([Antigravity plan changes](https://www.antigravity.google/blog/changes-to-antigravity-plans)). By comparison, Farbaek's model and sandbox allowances add up to about **44% to 55% of each price at list rates**. Older official Anthropic guidance put Max 5x at about 140 to 280 hours of Sonnet 4 a week, and that guidance is now outdated ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-throttles-claude-rate-limits-devs-call-foul)). Farbaek's $100 plan covers about 14 hours of agent API time a month (130 sessions at about 6 minutes each). The two measures are not the same, but a gap that large in scale cannot be closed by tuning prices. The custom tier faces the sharpest version of this. A 20-seat team using $3,000 a month of list-rate tokens would pay Anthropic about $3,400 on Claude Enterprise, against about $5,455 for the same tokens under Farbaek's margin formula. Anthropic negotiates volume discounts case by case ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), and that negotiation is the only route to closing the gap on tokens.

## Predictable metering, not bigger numbers, makes plans feel worth paying for

The backlash record shows what users punish: surprise charges, opaque limits, and cuts presented as increases. Cursor's CEO apologised in 2025 after a switch to dollar-based usage left users out of allowance within a few prompts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/07/cursor-apologizes-for-unclear-pricing-changes-that-upset-users/)). Anthropic's announced 25% increase to Claude Code weekly limits in September 2026 was in practice **a 17% cut** from the limits users then had, according to secondary reporting of Anthropic's posts ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-is-cutting-claude-codes-current-weekly-limits-by-17-percent/)). GitHub called flat premium requests unsustainable because a quick question and a multi-hour agent session cost the same ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2026-05-01-github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)). After Replit's effort-based pricing arrived, one user reported spending $1,000 in a week ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/18/replit_agent3_pricing/)), and v0's move to token credits set off a community thread asking Vercel to change the pricing back ([Vercel community](https://community.vercel.com/t/save-v0-change-the-pricing-back/12892)). Vendors have responded with cost confirmation before paid actions (Replit), cost shown after each message and free manual edits (Base44), and free or cheap planning (Replit's Free Mode, Base44's 0.3-credit Discuss mode) ([Replit AI billing](https://docs.replit.com/billing/ai-billing); [Base44 credits docs](https://docs.base44.com/Getting-Started/Credits)).

For Farbaek, the research supports a set of design choices, which are my recommendations rather than sourced facts. The first is to **meter in a published unit with a per-model rate card**, as v0 and Copilot do, drawn down by actual tokens and sandbox minutes, and to show "about N typical sessions" only as guidance. That absorbs the 30x run variance and the Gemini changes without re-cutting plans. A credit shown as dollars at list price would expose the 2.1x to 2.7x gap against Replit's and Copilot's face-value framing. The second is to **route by default to cheap models**. A Haiku 5.5 session costs about 2.4 cents, so 50 free planning or chat sessions cost about $1.20 a month and fit inside the $19.99 model budget, giving Farbaek its own version of Replit's Free Mode. Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 should draw down faster, the way Antigravity's shared pool does. The third is to **offer a two-month rollover** that lapses on cancellation, matching Lovable, Bolt and v0 ([Lovable plans and credits](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/plans-and-credits)). Because every allowance above is costed as fully used, each month's allowance can still be spent only once, so rollover gives up breakage, not margin. The one risk is a price rise between when credit is earned and when it is spent, and a rate card set on 2027 Gemini prices removes it. The fourth is to **sell top-ups at the plan's own rate**, using the same formula. A $10 pack funds $5.00 of cost (about 14 Sonnet sessions), and a $50 pack funds $27.00 (about 75). Paddle's 50¢ fee makes packs under $10 poor value, and a 12-month expiry matches Lovable and v0. The fifth is to show a usage meter with the share used and the reset time, a hard spend cap, and a cost estimate before any expensive task. The sixth is to **not charge for failed or self-inflicted retries, within a stated cap**. That needs to be funded explicitly, for example by holding back about 5% of each model budget. The seventh is to **include security checks at the $19.99 to $100 tiers**. Lovable reserves its security centre for Business and scheduled scans for Enterprise ([Lovable subscription plans](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/subscription-plans)), and a 2025 scan found 170 of 1,645 Lovable-built apps exposing data through missing row-level security ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/29/2025/the-hottest-new-vibe-coding-startup-lovable-is-a-sitting-duck-for-hackers)). The custom tier should sell committed spend with metered usage at about 1.82x cost and lead with governance, sandboxes and verification rather than tokens. Paddle prices high-volume deals by quote only ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)).

Several inputs need re-checking before prices go public. The pricing-page summary claimed that Claude Max includes $100 or $200 of monthly API credit, and that claim was not confirmed by a second source. All Devin Desktop figures come from secondary sources, because the official page returned an error. Bolt's Pro ladder, Base44's monthly prices and a claim that Cursor's bundle is at a half-price promotion are unverified. Lovable's daily chat allowance is temporary through October 31, 2026, and Copilot's flex credits can change at GitHub's discretion. The Gemini Priority rows came from a single extraction pass. Anthropic, Supabase, Resend and Paddle show no page dates. The 30% tokenizer uplift is applied as an assumption to a single sample session, and Gemini's tokenizer was not compared.

## Conclusion

The economics are the reverse of ordinary SaaS. At 200 subscribers, infrastructure is about 2% of the $19.99 price, and the variables that decide whether a plan is generous or stingy are the cache hit rate, model routing and Google's price calendar. Losing the cache on a Sonnet session multiplies its cost 7.4 times, while moving from Sonnet to Opus only doubles it, so a five-minute cache that expires while a user reviews a preview matters more than the choice of model. A rate card set on today's Gemini Flash price turns the $19.99 plan unprofitable on January 1. Because the margin rule forces every list-price token to sell for at least 2.1 times its cost, Farbaek cannot match Claude Max, Antigravity Ultra or Copilot Max on volume at the same price. Its plans have to sell what those products leave out: sandboxes, hosting and a database included, no surprise bills, rollover, no charge for failed attempts, and security checks on the tiers where competitors offer none.

Over time, the main way to grow included usage at the same price is a lower cost per token, not a lower margin. That comes from negotiated Anthropic volume pricing, aggressive caching and cheap-model routing. Changing processor helps less than it seems. Stripe direct through a US entity beats Paddle by about a point only on US cards, and on international cards it costs more (6.2% against Paddle's 6.0% at $50), before any tax filing fees ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing); [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). Even the $300 plan's model budget ($141.62) falls just short of the low end of Anthropic's $150 to $250 monthly average for enterprise Claude Code developers, so full-time professionals belong on the metered custom tier rather than a flat plan.
