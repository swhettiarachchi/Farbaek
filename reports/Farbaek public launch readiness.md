# Close five gaps before Farbaek opens publicly

Farbaek can launch publicly within days, but not on its current footing. Three gaps block it. First, production site checks run on Vercel Sandbox Hobby, which allows only non-commercial use. Its allowance is for the whole account (5 Active CPU hours, 10 concurrent sandboxes and 45-minute sessions a month), which covers about three trial users at the current 300-minute trial sandbox allowance. Second, there is no Terms of Service, Privacy Policy or Acceptable Use Policy, and no abuse or security contact. Third, sign-up has no bot check or disposable-email screen, yet each trial can spend about $3.70 of model usage on the owner's Anthropic key plus up to $1.70 of sandbox time. The fixes are cheap. Vercel Pro costs $20 a month with $20 of usage credit and brings the DPA the privacy policy needs. Five layers bound the AI exposure: Cloudflare Turnstile, the public-domain disposable-domain list, GoTrue's own rate limits (once they see the real client IP), an Anthropic workspace spend limit and a daily spend breaker in Postgres. Better Stack, UptimeRobot, Healthchecks.io and Cloudflare R2 provide uptime alerts, job heartbeats and encrypted, delete-locked off-site backups for $0. On the legal side, GDPR and UK GDPR reach Farbaek under Article 3(2) because it offers a service to people in Europe, so the policies should be written to that standard. CCPA does not apply at Farbaek's size, and Sri Lanka's PDPA controller duties start on 1 January 2027. Only the owner can do the account, key, money and legal-review steps. The policy pages, trial gate, spend breaker, database readiness check and backup wiring can be built in code this week. This report is drafting input, not legal advice. The liability, consumer-law and EU representative questions need a lawyer.

## GDPR's Article 3(2) reach sets the privacy policy's standard

Farbaek is a Sri Lankan sole proprietorship, but GDPR still applies. It offers a signed-up, English-language service to people in the EU, which brings it within Article 3(2). The policy's backbone is therefore the Article 13 list: who the controller is, the purpose and **lawful basis for each processing**, recipients, third-country transfers and their safeguards, retention periods, the full set of rights, the right to complain to a supervisory authority, and any automated decision-making ([GDPR Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)). A non-EU controller caught by Article 3(2) must also appoint an **EU representative** in writing. The exemption requires processing that is occasional, involves no large-scale special-category data and is unlikely to create risk, and all three must hold ([GDPR Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/)). One compliance vendor reads "occasional" as excluding ongoing services to EU residents ([GDPRLocal](https://gdprlocal.com/gdpr-article-27-gdpr-exemptions/)). That makes a representative likely, though no regulator text confirms it. UK GDPR is understood to carry a matching UK-representative rule, but this research did not verify it against ICO guidance.

The other two regimes are lighter today. CCPA covers a for-profit business only if it has **$26,625,000 in annual revenue**, buys, sells or shares the data of 100,000 or more consumers, or earns half its revenue from selling or sharing personal data ([Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/ccpa-applicability-guide); [Sprinto](https://sprinto.com/ccpa/)). Farbaek meets none of these, so a short paragraph saying it neither sells nor shares personal information is enough. Sri Lanka's PDPA No. 9 of 2022 had its start dates repeatedly deferred. A Presidential Extraordinary Gazette dated 22 July 2026 now sets **1 January 2027** as the start for Sections 2 and 3 of Part I (processing of personal data) and for Part III (controllers and processors) ([Ada Derana, 8 Aug 2026](https://adaderana.lk/news/cmsjsp2vt0005356q4pjfv4ih)). The Act applies to controllers domiciled in Sri Lanka and to foreign controllers serving people there. Once in force it brings written processor agreements, breach notification, cross-border transfer limits under Section 26, and fines of up to **LKR 10 million per breach**, doubled for repeat breaches ([HashtagCoders](https://hashtagcoders.lk/blogs/data-privacy-laws-sri-lanka-2026)). The published report frames the Gazette around public authorities. Whether it covers private controllers, and when Part II (individual rights) starts, needs Sri Lankan counsel. Writing to the GDPR standard now covers most of what the PDPA will ask.

The lawful-basis map is short. Article 6(1)(b) (contract) covers almost everything Farbaek does for the user, and Recital 49 names network and information security as a legitimate interest ([GDPR Art. 6](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/); [GDPR Recital 49](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-49/)).

| Data | Lawful basis |
|---|---|
| Account data (email, name, session) | Contract, Art. 6(1)(b) |
| Prompts, project files, AI output, sandbox runs, published sites | Contract: the user asks for the work, and it needs Claude, Gemini and the sandbox |
| Security logs, rate limiting, hashed trial-eligibility signals | Legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) |
| Trial reminders and service email | Contract or legitimate interests |
| Marketing email | Consent |
| Tax records once payments start | Legal obligation, Art. 6(1)(c) |

The sub-processor list is where the policy can most easily say something untrue. Anthropic's Commercial Terms state that **Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content**. They give the customer ownership of outputs, and Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days by default. Content flagged by its safety classifiers can be kept for up to two years ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms); [Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-personal-data)). Google's protection is conditional. On unpaid Gemini API use, Google uses submitted content to improve its products and human reviewers may read it. On Paid Services it does not. Products offered to users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland **must use Paid Services only**, and users must be 18 or older ([Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). Vercel's DPA, with the 2021 SCCs and the UK IDTA, applies to Pro and Enterprise customers only ([Vercel DPA](https://vercel.com/legal/dpa)). The sentence "our AI providers don't train on your work" is therefore true only if the Gemini key sits on a billing-enabled project, or Gemini is switched off, and Vercel is on Pro.

The public sub-processor page should list:

- Anthropic (AI drafting);
- Google (Gemini, paid tier only);
- Vercel (sandboxes);
- Hostinger (the VPS that holds the app, database, auth and backups);
- Spaceship/Spacemail (email);
- Cloudflare (DNS, and soon Turnstile and R2).

Each entry needs its purpose, the data it handles, its location and its transfer safeguard. Hostinger's DPA and data-centre country, Spacemail's DPA and Cloudflare's DPA are still unconfirmed. On transfers, the EDPB's three-part test suggests an EU user typing into a Sri Lankan service is probably not itself a "transfer". Farbaek's onward flows to US processors probably are, and they need SCCs or Data Privacy Framework cover ([EDPB Guidelines 05/2021](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_guidelines_05-2021_interplay_between_the_application_of_art3-chapter_v_of_the_gdpr_v2_en_0.pdf)). Sri Lanka has no EU adequacy decision ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en)), so the policy should say plainly where Farbaek operates.

Deletion and export need care, because the code already makes a promise that the planned backups would break. The ICO says erasure must reach backups. Backup copies can wait until they are overwritten on a set schedule, provided they are held **"beyond use"** and people are told clearly what happens ([ICO, right to erasure](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-dp/guide-to-the-uk-gdpr/individual-rights/right-to-erasure)). Portability means data "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format", and rights requests get a one-month answer ([GDPR Art. 20](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-20-gdpr/); [GDPR Art. 12](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/)).

Farbaek's delete-account screen tells people their nightly backups are overwritten within 14 days (`src/components/app/delete-account.tsx`). That matches today's 14 local dumps (`scripts/vps/db-backup.sh`), but not the 30-day bucket lock and 35-to-45-day expiry recommended for off-site copies below. One of the two must change before R2 goes live. The better choice is to keep the 30-day lock and change the wording to "within 35 days", because two weeks is a short window to notice silent corruption. A per-project export route exists (`src/app/app/projects/[projectId]/export/route.ts`). This review found no account-wide export of profile, task, approval and ProofPack data as JSON alongside project ZIPs.

Cookies need no banner. The ePrivacy Directive and the UK's PECR exempt storage that is strictly necessary for a service the user asked for. The ICO treats security cookies as exempt and preference cookies as likely exempt. It still recommends describing them ([ICO, cookies](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/); [Directive 2002/58/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02002L0058-20091219)). With only the Supabase session cookies and the workspace-choice cookie, a banner would wrongly suggest tracking exists. A "Cookies and local storage" section listing each real name, purpose and lifetime is enough. The one grey area is a long-lived refresh-token cookie. The Article 29 Working Party's opinion treats persistent cross-session login cookies as not automatically exempt ([WP194](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2012/wp194_en.pdf)), so a modest lifetime lowers the risk.

## Terms and AUP must pass Anthropic's and Google's rules on to users

Much of the Terms of Service is decided upstream. Anthropic lets customers use Claude to power their own products, but forbids reselling access without approval. Customers and their users must follow Anthropic's Usage Policy. Customers must also tell users that **factual statements in AI output should not be relied on without independent checking** (clauses A.1, D.2, D.3 and D.4). Customers own outputs, and Anthropic gives 30 days' notice of term changes ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). Google's Gemini terms add the 18+ rule. Farbaek's terms should therefore:

- set the minimum age at 18;
- leave users owning their inputs and generated code, with Farbaek passing on any rights it holds;
- take only the licence needed to host, run, back up and publish;
- say that AI output can be wrong, insecure or infringing, and must be reviewed before it is relied on or published, which matches Farbaek's approval-gated flow;
- avoid any "guaranteed" wording.

The trial clause must describe what the code actually does: 14 days, no card, what happens at the end, one trial per person, and the published limits.

Choosing Sri Lankan law and the Colombo courts is a reasonable default, but it cannot remove protections from European consumers. Rome I keeps the mandatory protections of the consumer's home law. Brussels Ia lets consumers sue at home ([Rome I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32008R0593); [Brussels Ia](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32012R1215)). The Digital Content Directive covers services paid for with personal data, so a free trial is not exempt ([Directive 2019/770](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/770/oj)). Unfair non-negotiated terms do not bind consumers ([Directive 93/13/EEC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:31993L0013)). The liability cap and disclaimers must say explicitly that non-excludable consumer rights survive, and that clause is the one to send to a lawyer. A sole proprietor carries unlimited personal liability. Incorporating before scaling is a common step and worth discussing with an accountant.

The Acceptable Use Policy should follow Vercel's six-part structure: scope, prohibited activities, a section on AI services, DMCA, violations and changes. Vercel's AI section bans bypassing safety measures and stripping provenance markers ([Vercel AUP](https://vercel.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy)). Farbaek should add explicit bans on malware hosting, cryptomining, scanning from sandboxes, multi-account trial farming and squatting on subdomain names. Lovable is a useful operating benchmark. It takes phishing reports at an abuse address with evidence, keeps reporters confidential, routes copyright complaints to a separate form, and acts within 24 hours on confirmed violations ([Lovable trust and safety](https://lovable.dev/faq/trust-and-safety/report)). Farbaek already has the mechanism: the site server rechecks each name every few seconds, so a takedown shows quickly (`src/app/site-serve/[[...path]]/route.ts`). What's missing is a published channel. The public contact config lists only hello@ and support@ (`src/config/contact.ts`), and `security.txt` is waiting on a real address (`docs/architecture/current-state.md`).

Two takedown regimes apply. Under US law, registering a **DMCA designated agent** costs USD 6 for three years and protects US safe harbour. The provider must remove material "expeditiously" after a valid notice ([US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/); [Orrick](https://orrick.com/Insights/2016/12/New-DMCA-Designated-Agent-Requirements-Effective-December-2016)). The fee figure comes from 2016-17 sources. The statute also requires a repeat-infringer policy ([17 U.S.C. §512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)). In the EU, the Digital Services Act requires every intermediary to publish points of contact and content-moderation terms, and requires non-EU providers to appoint a legal representative (Articles 11 to 15). Hosting services must also run notice-and-action and give a **statement of reasons** whenever they restrict content (Articles 16 to 18) ([Regulation (EU) 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)). The small-business exclusion in Article 19 sits in the online-platform section, so these hosting duties appear to reach micro providers too ([Presencis](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/dsa/applicability/)). That reading comes from the regulation's structure, not from a regulator's statement. Some commercial services cover both the GDPR and DSA representative roles. Their cost is the largest unknown in this report.

The EU AI Act adds a light layer. Its Article 50 transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026, even though the Digital Omnibus pushed the high-risk rules back to December 2027 ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/)). Users must be told they are dealing with AI unless it is obvious, and synthetic text must carry machine-readable marking where technically feasible ([AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). An AI app builder plainly meets the "obvious" test. Labelling drafts "Drafted by AI (Claude)" is still cheap and fits the honesty rules. Whether generated code needs marking is unresolved. Systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to add it ([Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026)), so a launch now gets no grace period. Recording model and timestamp provenance on each AI change, which ProofPacks already partly do, is the prudent step. Deepfake and public-interest-text labelling passes to users who publish such content, through the AUP.

## Vercel Hobby can serve about three full trials a month

The sandbox decision is settled by Vercel's own terms. Hobby includes **5 Active CPU hours, 420 GB-hours of memory, 5,000 creations and 10 concurrent sandboxes a month**, with a 45-minute session limit. When the quota runs out, sandbox creation pauses for the rest of the 30-day window rather than billing ([Vercel Sandbox pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-sandbox/pricing)). Hobby is also "non-commercial, personal use only" ([Vercel Hobby Plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby); [Vercel Fair Use Guidelines](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). Farbaek's production checks run on Hobby today (`docs/architecture/current-state.md`). The trial is shaped for Pro: 300 provisioned minutes, 2 concurrent sessions, 120-minute sessions and both sandbox sizes (`src/config/sandbox.ts`, `src/core/billing/catalog.ts`). At a typical 33% CPU use, Hobby's CPU hours give the whole platform about 15 small-sandbox hours a month, which is three full trials. Five workspaces running two checks each would fill the concurrency limit. Hitting the cap would stop every user's checks for up to a month.

Pro removes all three limits for **$20 a month, including $20 of usage credit**. That credit covers all Vercel usage from the first unit, not just sandboxes. Beyond it, Pro charges $0.128 per Active CPU hour and $0.0212 per GB-hour of provisioned memory, with 10,000 concurrent sandboxes and 24-hour sessions ([Vercel Pro Plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan); [Vercel Sandbox pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-sandbox/pricing)). Active CPU excludes time spent waiting on the network, which suits checks that spend most of their time installing packages. Memory bills for every wall-clock minute. Farbaek's plan economics already assume Vercel Pro as a fixed cost and price a sandbox hour at $0.149 (`src/core/billing/allowance.ts`), so upgrading matches the pricing model rather than adding a new cost.

| Check (1 vCPU / 2 GB) | Vercel Pro | E2B or Daytona | Modal | Cloudflare Containers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 min wall, 40 s CPU | $0.0028 | $0.0028 | $0.0040 | $0.0027 |
| 3 min wall, 20 s CPU (mostly install) | $0.0028 | $0.0041 | $0.0059 | $0.0033 |
| Runaway: 24 h at 100% CPU | $4.09 | $1.99 | $2.86 | $3.11 |

*Costs are derived from each vendor's published rates ([E2B](https://e2b.dev/pricing); [Daytona](https://www.daytona.io/pricing); [Modal](https://modal.com/pricing); [Cloudflare Containers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/pricing/)).*

E2B bills about **$0.083 an hour** for 1 vCPU and 2 GiB, whether or not the CPU is busy. It costs the same as Vercel Pro at about 32% average CPU use and less above that. Its free Hobby tier is a one-time $100 credit with 20 concurrent sandboxes and 1-hour sessions. Pro is $150 a month before usage, with no included credit ([E2B pricing](https://e2b.dev/pricing); [E2B billing](https://docs.e2b.dev/billing)). This research found no terms saying whether E2B Hobby allows commercial use. Daytona charges the same per-second rates as E2B, gives $200 of free compute and offers startup credits of up to $50,000, which are worth applying for ([Daytona pricing](https://www.daytona.io/pricing)). For a launch of this size, Vercel Pro is the cheapest option that complies with the terms, and it keeps the code path that already passes `pnpm sandbox:smoke`. The existing E2B runtime stays as a fallback.

The trial allowance is a money decision for the owner, but the numbers point one way. None of the app builders reviewed publishes a sandbox-minutes limit. They all cap free use with a small daily grant under a ceiling: Lovable gives 5 build credits a day up to 30 a month, Bolt gives 1M tokens a month with a 300K daily cap, and v0 allows 7 messages a day plus $5 of one-time credit ([Lovable](https://lovable.dev/pricing); [Bolt](https://bolt.new/pricing); [v0](https://v0.app/pricing)). The daily cap is the point, because it stops one farmed account spending everything in one sitting.

At today's 300 minutes on the standard size, a trial that maxes out its CPU costs about $1.70, so 1,000 such sign-ups cost about $1,700. The recommended trial shape is:

- 120 minutes in total;
- small size only;
- 1 concurrent session;
- 45-minute sessions;
- about 30 minutes a day.

That cuts the worst case to about **$0.34 per trial** and still allows about 60 two-minute checks. Any session left open, such as a preview, counts its full wall-clock time. Farbaek already records `vcpuSeconds`, `gibSeconds` and active execution time per session (`src/core/sandbox/usage.ts`), so the first week's real numbers should set the final figure.

Two platform guardrails matter more than the per-user number. First, lower Vercel Spend Management from its **$200 default** notification ([Vercel Pro Plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/pro-plan)). Second, add Farbaek's own monthly sandbox counter, because the research could not confirm that Vercel's "pause projects" action blocks sandbox creation. Hard timeouts must also stay on the server. A single runaway Pro sandbox at 8 vCPU and 16 GB for 24 hours would cost about $33.

## Each farmed trial is worth about five dollars, so screen it at sign-up

The exposure comes from Farbaek's own allowance formula. The trial gets a month of Starter's AI usage, which `allowanceFor(9.99, 5)` puts at about **$3.70 of model usage at list prices** (including the retry hold-back), plus 5 sandbox hours (`src/core/billing/catalog.ts`, `src/core/billing/allowance.ts`). One honest user costs cents. A script that creates 100 verified accounts a day could cost about $500 a day. The source has no CAPTCHA, Turnstile or disposable-email code today. Stripe's guidance on trial abuse names the signals to watch: bursts of accounts from one IP, clustering by subnet or network, hosting and VPN ranges, and disposable domains. It also advises measuring sign-ups before enforcing blocks ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/how-to-prevent-free-trial-abuse-in-saas-and-ai-products)).

**Turnstile is the first layer, and GoTrue should verify it.** Self-hosted Supabase Auth reads `GOTRUE_SECURITY_CAPTCHA_ENABLED`, `GOTRUE_SECURITY_CAPTCHA_PROVIDER` and `GOTRUE_SECURITY_CAPTCHA_SECRET` ([supabase/auth configuration.go](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/supabase/auth/master/internal/conf/configuration.go)), and `signInWithOtp` takes the widget token as `options.captchaToken` ([supabase-js types](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/supabase/supabase-js/master/packages/core/auth-js/src/lib/types.ts)). Turnstile tokens are valid for 300 seconds and **can be validated only once** ([Turnstile server-side validation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/)). If GoTrue and the server action both verified the same token, the second check would fail. GoTrue-side enforcement is the safer choice because it also guards the public `/auth/v1` endpoints, which an attacker can call directly without going through Next.js. The free plan has unlimited challenges, but Ephemeral IDs, which link activity across sessions, are Enterprise only ([Turnstile plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)). The free plan therefore gives a bot-or-human verdict, not device linking. Three details need attention:

- the CSP must allow `challenges.cloudflare.com`;
- the widget must be reset after every attempt;
- the provider string, presumably `turnstile`, should be confirmed in GoTrue's source before deploying.

**Rate limits only work if GoTrue sees the real client IP.** `GOTRUE_RATE_LIMIT_HEADER` is empty by default ([configuration.go](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/supabase/auth/master/internal/conf/configuration.go)). When a Next.js server action calls GoTrue over the internal network, every request appears to come from the app container. All the per-IP buckets then collapse into one, and one abuser can lock out everyone. The fix has three parts:

- set the header on GoTrue;
- forward the client IP that Caddy recorded, never one the client supplied;
- route `/auth/v1` only through Caddy, which overwrites the header.

The project-wide email-sent cap is global, so setting it too low lets an attacker block every real sign-in for an hour. About 60 to 120 an hour, with `GOTRUE_SMTP_MAX_FREQUENCY=60s` per address, is a reasonable starting point. The default link lifetime of 86,400 seconds should come down to between 15 minutes and an hour. These values are judgement calls, not vendor recommendations. A Caddy limiter is possible through the third-party `caddy-ratelimit` module, which can bucket IPv6 by /64 prefix, but it needs a custom Caddy build and loses its state on restart ([mholt/caddy-ratelimit](https://github.com/mholt/caddy-ratelimit)). Reusing Farbaek's existing request limiter in the sign-in action needs no new dependency.

**The trial grant is the right place to screen, not sign-in.** The standard disposable-domain list is public domain (CC0). It has no official npm package, and its maintainers say plainly that it contains false positives ([disposable-email-domains](https://github.com/disposable-email-domains/disposable-email-domains)). Copying the file into the repo, with the upstream commit recorded, fits the rule on pinned dependencies. A listed domain should still be able to sign in. It gets a withheld trial with an honest reason and a way forward, such as using another email or contacting support, which keeps the "no dead ends" rule.

One trial per person needs a normalised email: lowercase, the `+tag` removed, and dots removed for Gmail, which ignores them ([Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/7436150)). Store it as a keyed hash that survives account deletion. Supabase's Before User Created hook receives the sign-up IP, so it can cap new accounts per IP prefix per day ([Supabase hook docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/before-user-created-hook)). Responses should be graduated: one or two trials per prefix per day pass, three to five get a delayed or reduced start, and more than that is withheld. Offices, universities and mobile networks put many real people behind one IPv4 address, so IP alone should never trigger a ban. Invasive device fingerprinting isn't needed.

**Provider caps are a backstop, and Farbaek needs its own daily breaker.** Anthropic Console organisations can set monthly organisation and workspace spend limits that are checked on every request, with email notifications at chosen amounts ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9796807-creating-and-managing-workspaces-in-the-claude-console)). This came from a search summary and should be confirmed in the Console. The Admin API reports usage in 1-minute buckets, usually within 5 minutes ([Anthropic Usage and Cost API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/usage-cost-api)), but setting limits through the API is early-access only ([Spend Limits API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/admin/spend_limits/create.md)). Gemini's Project Spend Caps take effect about **10 minutes late, and any overage in that window is billed to the account** ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/more-control-over-gemini-api-costs/)). Google Cloud budgets only send alerts unless a service supports spend-cap budgets ([Google Cloud budgets](https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/budgets)). None of these offers a daily cap for a Console organisation.

The design that fits Farbaek's write path, through `use-cases.ts` and then the `app_*` SQL functions, has four parts:

- **Reserve, then settle.** Before each AI call, reserve the rate-card estimate at `max_tokens`. Settle the actual cost afterwards. Expire stale reservations at the estimate.
- **Breaker per group.** Trial, paid and internal traffic each get a daily soft and hard limit. The soft limit emails the owner. The hard limit trips only the trial group.
- **Fail closed.** If the ledger can't be read or written, refuse trial AI calls.
- **Kill switch and honest errors.** Office Space gets an audited kill switch. An Anthropic limit returns HTTP 400 and a Gemini cap returns 429, and both should map to an honest "free-trial AI is paused until 00:00 UTC" banner rather than a generic error.

A separate Anthropic workspace and key for trial traffic, with its own hard monthly limit, means even a bug in Farbaek's metering cannot spend the paid customers' headroom.

## Four free services watch the VPS and keep backups off it

Monitoring costs nothing and takes about a day to set up. **Better Stack's free tier** includes 10 monitors and 10 heartbeats at 3-minute intervals, with email and Slack alerts and SSL and domain-expiry monitoring on all plans ([Better Stack vs UptimeRobot](https://betterstack.com/uptime-robot-alternative); [Better Stack pricing](https://betterstack.com/pricing)). **UptimeRobot's free plan** gives 50 monitors at 5-minute intervals. Its terms, changed on 26 May 2026, now say it is "available for any use, including commercial and business use", reversing the late-2024 personal-use restriction ([UptimeRobot Terms](https://uptimerobot.com/terms/)). Its pricing page still calls Free "good for hobby" projects and contradicts itself on free SSL monitoring ([UptimeRobot pricing](https://uptimerobot.com/pricing/)), so Better Stack should own the certificate and domain-expiry alerts. Running two vendors is sensible. Freshping disabled its free accounts on 6 March 2026 after two months' notice ([notifier.so](https://notifier.so/guides/freshping-shutdown/)), and Cloudflare Health Checks are not on the Free plan ([Cloudflare Health Checks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/health-checks/)).

The monitor target has one gap. `/api/health` deliberately touches no database or provider (`src/app/api/health/route.ts`). A keyword check for `"ok":true` would therefore pass while Postgres or GoTrue is down. Keep that liveness route for Docker and the deploy script, and add a readiness route that runs `select 1` and fails closed, as the second monitor's target.

**Healthchecks.io covers the systemd timers.** Its free Hobbyist plan includes 20 checks ([Healthchecks.io pricing](https://healthchecks.io/pricing/)). Its documented pattern pings `/start` in `ExecStartPre` and `/${EXIT_STATUS}` in `ExecStopPost`, both prefixed with `-` so a monitoring outage can't fail the job. A missed ping raises an alert after the grace period ([Healthchecks.io systemd guide](https://healthchecks.io/docs/monitoring_systemd_tasks/)). Five or six checks cover the setup:

- the nightly dump;
- the off-site copy;
- the weekly restore test;
- the hourly jobs timer;
- an hourly disk check that pings `/fail` above about 80% use.

Each ping URL is a bearer secret and belongs in a mode-0600 `EnvironmentFile`. The backup script already uses `set -euo pipefail`. When no off-site remote is configured, though, it prints a note and exits 0 (`scripts/vps/db-backup.sh`). Once R2 exists, a missing remote should count as a failure, or the heartbeat will report success while nothing leaves the server. For application errors, Sentry's free Developer plan (one user, 5,000 errors) or Better Stack's free 100,000 exceptions a month will do ([Sentry pricing](https://sentry.io/pricing/); [Better Stack pricing](https://betterstack.com/pricing)). Either needs an SDK install, which the owner must approve.

**Cloudflare R2 holds months of dumps for free.** The free tier includes 10 GB-month of storage, 1 million Class A and 10 million Class B operations, free egress and free deletes ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). At about 50 MB a dump, 30 daily copies take roughly 1.5 GB. The server needs an **Object Read & Write token scoped to one bucket** ([R2 API tokens](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/tokens/)), and rclone with `no_check_bucket`, which the script already passes as `--s3-no-check-bucket` on rclone 1.71.1 ([Cloudflare rclone example](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/examples/rclone/)).

That token can also delete objects. A compromised VPS could therefore wipe the off-site history. R2 doesn't support S3 versioning or Object Lock ([R2 S3 compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/api/)). Its own **bucket locks** do the same job: they block deletes and overwrites for a set age, even with a valid token, and they override lifecycle rules ([R2 bucket locks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/bucket-locks/)). Changing a lock needs admin rights that the server's token does not have. The script names each dump with a timestamp down to the second, so the lock's no-overwrite rule won't collide with it. A 30-day lock plus a 35-day lifecycle expiry ([R2 object lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)) is the right shape, together with the deletion-wording change described earlier. Whether R2 needs a card on file even on the free tier is unclear. Backblaze B2, which offers 10 GB free and a compliance-mode Object Lock that no user can remove, is the fallback ([Backblaze pricing](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing); [Backblaze Object Lock](https://www.backblaze.com/docs/cloud-storage-object-lock)).

Encrypt before upload. **rclone crypt** encrypts contents with XSalsa20-Poly1305 and also encrypts file names ([rclone crypt](https://rclone.org/crypt/)). If the crypt remote is named `offsite` and wraps an `r2:` remote, the script's existing `offsite:<bucket>/database/` path keeps working with no code change, and the whole bucket gets the lock because its prefixes are now encrypted. The crypt password in rclone's config is only "lightly obscured". The alternative is `age`, which encrypts to a public key so the server can never decrypt old backups, at the cost of one extra script step ([age](https://github.com/FiloSottile/age)). Either way, the decryption secret must live off the VPS, in the owner's password manager and one offline copy. A monthly drill on the owner's PC should download the latest object, decrypt it and run `pg_restore --list`, proving the copy can be restored, not just that it exists.

## Conclusion

The serious launch risks are operational, not regulatory. A fine for a missing EU representative is possible. A Hobby quota tripping on day three and stopping every user's checks for a month is likely. So is a script farming $5 trials faster than the owner can read alerts. Both cost less to prevent than a lawyer's hour: $20 a month for Vercel Pro, and $0 for Turnstile, the CC0 domain list, an Anthropic workspace limit and a Postgres ledger. The legal work also depends on the code, not the other way round. Every honest sentence in the privacy policy, from "providers don't train on your work" to "backups are gone within 35 days", is true only once a specific account setting or code path exists. The order is therefore: change the settings, build the controls, then write the policy from what the system actually does, and have a lawyer read it.

The next deadlines are close. The EU AI Act marking grace period ends on 2 December 2026, though a new launch probably can't use it. Sri Lanka's PDPA controller duties start on 1 January 2027. Paid plans will bring withdrawal rights, tax records and a payment processor to add to the sub-processor list. A trial gate, spend breaker and takedown log built now will carry over unchanged to paid accounts.
